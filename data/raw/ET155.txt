Moyale
Moyale is a town straddling the border between Ethiopia and Kenya. Ethiopian Moyale (north of the border) and Kenyan Moyale (south of the border).
Understand
Moyale is a border town in southern Ethiopia, on the international frontier with Kenya. The town is divided by the border, with an Ethiopian settlement on the northern side and a Kenyan settlement on the southern side. Moyale is an important transport and trading centre on the main route connecting Addis Ababa with Nairobi and northern Kenya.
Moyale lies in a dry, semi-arid part of the Horn of Africa. The surrounding landscape consists mainly of open scrubland, grassland and low hills, with vegetation adapted to limited rainfall.
The town's location on an international border gives it an important role in cross-border trade. Livestock, agricultural products, consumer goods and other commodities are traded in the wider Moyale area.
The population is culturally diverse, with Oromo and Somali communities among those living in the surrounding region. Oromo, Somali and other local languages are spoken, while Amharic is widely used on the Ethiopian side.
Get in
Buses from Addis Ababa to Moyale runs daily. Companies Selam bus (3900 birr) or Africa bus (3800 birr), as of May 2026, can be bought in their ticket office shacks at St. Estifanos Church (9°00'44.9"N 38°45'47.6"E, up the stairs in front of the church, turn left, look for corrugated iron shacks!), cards not accepted, cash/QR only.
Get around
Bajaj three-wheelers, taxis and minibuses are common within the town. Walking is practical in central areas.

See and do
Moyale is a major border crossing between Ethiopia and Kenya. There is a One Stop Border Point (OSBP) where travelers can process paperwork for exit of one country into entrance of the other country all within one building.
Buy
There are banks on both the Kenyan and the Ethiopian side. The KCB on the Kenyan side has an ATM. Equity Bank is also available on the Kenyan side, Equity ATM charges US$5 per withdrawal. On either side of the border there are money changers, their rate is at least 10% worse than official rate.

Stay safe
Security in the area has improved. There is occasional ethnic fighting between the Gabra and Borana, such as the 2005 Turbi massacre. This violence shouldn't be directed at foreigners, but it's good to check the news and inquire about the local situation before departing to Moyale.
