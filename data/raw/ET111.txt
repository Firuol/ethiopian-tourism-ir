Document ID: ET111
Title: Negash (AI-Nejashi) Village: Sanctuary of the First Hijra and Enduring Legacy of lnterfaith Tolerance
URL: https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/7015/
Source: UNESCO World Heritage Centre
Topic: tentative-list
License: CC BY-SA IGO 3.0

Justification of Outstanding Universal Value
Negash (AI-Nejashi) Village possesses Outstanding Universal Value as a site directly associated with the First Hijra (615 CE), an event of foundational importance in Islamic history and of exceptional significance in the history of interreligious relations. The sanctuary granted by the Christian Aksumite Kingdom to early Muslim migrants represents one of the earliest documented instances of ethical asylum, peaceful coexistence, and principled protection of religious freedom. This event has had a profound and enduring influence on Islamic civilization and on global conceptions of tolerance and moral responsibility.
The property bears exceptional testimony to the early establishment and continuity of Islam in Africa, expressed through its historic mosque, cemetery, and living settlement. These attributes illustrate the adaptation and transmission of Islamic religious traditions within a distinctive Ethiopian cultural, political, and environmental context, reflecting sustained interaction between lslamic and Christian societies over more than fourteen centuries.
As a living religious and cultural landscape, Negash village integrates tangible heritage-sacred architecture, burial grounds, and spatial organization - with intangible heritage, including pilgrimage practices, ritual observance, oral traditions, and long-standing community custodianship. Together, these attributes convey universal values of tolerance, compassion, and intercultural dialogue, underscoring the site's enduring relevance to humanity.
Criterion (ii): Negash (AI-Nejashi) Village demonstrates an important interchange of human values. For over more than thirteen centuries, Negash village has functioned continuously as a living religious and cultural center, reflecting the long-term outcomes of an early intercultural exchange between the Aksumite Kingdom and the first Muslim migrants. The property materializes the transmission and adaptation of Islamic religions practices beyond the Arabian Peninsula within an African cultural context, while also demonstrating the ethical and institutional values of protection and hospitality embedded in Aksumite governance. This sustained interaction resulted in the establishment of enduring religious institutions and cultural traditions that illustrate a significant interchange of human values between Africa and the early Islamic world, expressed through the site's religious architecture, burial traditions, and living settlement pattern.
Criterion (iii): The property bears exceptional testimony to a cultural tradition and to a living civilization. Negash Village - provides an exceptional testimony to the earliest phase of Islam outside the Arabian Peninsula and to its enduring presence in Africa. The mosque, cemetery, and village collectively embody the continuity of Islamic belief, practice, and community life since the 7th century CE. Unlike later monumental Islamic centers, Negash reflects the formative stage of Islam characterized by migration, protection, and peaceful coexistence, making it an irreplaceable witness to a foundational cultural and religious tradition.
Criterion (vi): Negash (AI-Nejashi) Village is directly and tangibly associated with events and living traditions of global significance. The site is directly associated with the First Hijra, one of the most significant events in Islamic history, marking the first organized migration of Muslims and establishing a moral precedent for asylum and interfaith respect. The protection offered by the Aksumite ruler kingdom constitutes a landmark episode in global history, symbolizing ethical leadership and intercultural solidarity. The site remains a living focus of pilgrimage and religious memory, maintaining an unbroken association with this event through ritual practice and collective remembrance.
Statements of authenticity and/or integrity
Authenticity
Negash (AI-Nejashi) village retains a high degree of authenticity in location, setting, function, and spirit. The site continues to serve its original religious and commemorative purposes, reinforced by enduring pilgrimage traditions and ritual practices. While some architectural elements have undergone repair or renewal, these interventions reflect traditional building practices and religious needs, rather than reconstruction or reinterpretation. Oral traditions, collective memory, and custodianship by the local community further reinforce the authenticity of the property's association with the First Hijra.
Integrity
The proposed property includes all elements necessary to express its Outstanding Universal Value: the historic place of the original mosque, burial grounds associated with the first Muslim migrants, and the living village that sustains religious and cultural practices. The spatial relationships between these elements remain legible and meaningful, preserving the site's historical and spiritual coherence. Ongoing religious use and community stewardship have ensured continuity while maintaining the essential attributes that convey its significance.
Comparison with other similar properties
Within Ethiopia, the World Heritage property of Harar Jugol represents a later phase of lslamic urban development from the sixteenth century onward. Negash differs fundamentally in chronology, origin, and meaning, as it predates Harar by over eight centuries and derives its significance from the First Hijra rather than urban, political, or commercial development.
Across Africa, sites such as Kairouan, Kilwa Kisiwani, Songo Mnara, and Lamu Old. Town reflect the expansion and institutional consolidation of Islam through architecture, trade, and political authority. Negash predates these sites and is distinct in character, having originated from an act of sanctuary rather than imperial expansion or commerce.
Beyond Africa, comparison may be drawn with Medina, the destination of the Prophet Muhammad's Hijra, which represents the internal formation of the first Muslim community. Negash, by contrast, represents the earliest outward migration of Islam into another cultural and civilizational sphere. Few lslamic sites worldwide combine such early chronology, direct association with events contemporary with the Prophet Muhammad, and uninterrupted continuity of religious practice.
Taken together, these comparisons demonstrate that Negash (Al-Nejashi) Village occupies a unique position globally as the earliest Islamic settlement in Africa, founded on principles of asylum, peaceful coexistence, and interreligious respect. No other property presents the same combination of antiquity, ethical significance, and living continuity.
